Visual Identity

The Strategus visual identity is a complete, interconnected system. Every element, the wordmark, the mark, the color palette, the typography, is built to work individually and together across the platform, campaign creative, sales materials, and reporting.

The system follows a clear architecture: one confident master brand that every team and channel can work within, without fragmenting. The mark anchors the system; the palette gives it range; the typefaces give it clarity.

Strategus runs programmatic Connected TV for brands and agencies, so the identity has to feel as expert and dependable as the service. This is the balance the system is engineered to hold.

Logo & mark

What to avoid

The logo is supplied as final artwork. Don’t redraw, re-space, or restyle it. These are the most common ways the identity gets weakened.

✕
Don’t stretch or distort
Always scale proportionally. Never stretch, squash, or skew the logo.
✕
Don’t recolor
Use only approved logo colors. Don’t apply gradients, secondary palette colors, or off-brand tints.
✕
Don’t add effects
No shadows, glows, outlines, or bevels. The logo stays flat.
✕
Don’t rotate
Keep the logo level. Never set it on an angle or rotate the mark.
✕
Don’t use low contrast
Don’t place the dark logo on dark or busy backgrounds. Use a reversed version when one is available.
✕
Don’t rebuild the wordmark
Never retype “Strategus” in another font. Always use the supplied logo artwork.
Color

A palette built for function, with room for character.

The Strategus color system leads with a confident Primary Blue, modern, technical, and optimistic, the color of screens lit up and campaigns running. It’s balanced by Dark Blue and Black for depth and legibility, so the brand reads as expert without feeling cold.

The system is structured for range. The primary palette anchors all core communication. The secondary palette, Orange, Purple, Pale Green, Green, and Fuscia, gives data viz, charts, and campaign creative room to differentiate without breaking cohesion. Color usage must always maintain sufficient contrast to meet WCAG AA standards.

Color

Primary palette

The primary palette is the anchor of the brand system. Primary Blue, Dark Blue, Black, and White appear across all Strategus communications and establish the visual baseline that every department and touchpoint builds from.

Color

Secondary palette

The secondary palette provides range for departmental differentiation, campaign work, and communications contexts where the primary palette alone would be insufficiently expressive. Secondary colors are always used within the primary system, never independently of it.

Each secondary color maintains sufficient contrast when paired with white or black text, ensuring accessibility standards are met regardless of usage context.

Color

Color combinations & accessibility

Every color combination in the system has been tested against WCAG 2.1 contrast requirements. Every approved pairing meets at least AA, and most reach AAA, for both normal and large text. Never use unapproved combinations or reduce contrast for aesthetic reasons.

Typography

Typography is an essential element of our identity and a powerful tool for achieving consistent expression in our communication.

Two typefaces carry the whole system: PolySans for display and headlines, Inter for body. Clear hierarchy does the work: a reader should scan any page, deck, or dashboard and immediately grasp what it is, what matters, and what to do next.

PolySans is a contemporary grotesk with a confident, slightly humanist character. Its even rhythm and open apertures give Strategus headlines presence without shouting, modern, technical, and approachable, the way the brand sounds.

It pairs with Inter for body copy: a workhorse screen typeface engineered for legibility at every size, from dashboard data to long-form copy. Together they cover display, headline, label, and body across every Strategus surface.

Typography

Typographic usage

Four levels, no exceptions. The system defines four typographic roles, display headlines, section headlines, eyebrow labels, and body copy, each mapped to a specific typeface, weight, size range, tracking, and leading. Mixing roles, weights, or sizes outside this system undermines hierarchy.

PolySans for leadership. PolySans carries all large headline and display roles. It is set in sentence case, tracked tight, and never used at body sizes. Headlines are never set in all caps.

No personality fonts. No decorative, script, or display-only typefaces are permitted within the brand system. Functional elegance is the standard.

Pentatonic
Formula:
an = a0 × r(n−1)
an = The type size / term in the sequence
a0 = The base type size, 12px
r = 2(1/5)
n = The step in the scale. e.g. step 1 = 12px

Google fallback font

When PolySans is unavailable but web fonts can be loaded, in vendor-built digital tools, third-party platforms, or environments where PolySans licensing cannot be guaranteed, DM Sans is the designated Google Fonts fallback.

DM Sans is a low-contrast geometric sans-serif optimized for on-screen readability. It shares PolySans's clean, neutral structure, making it the closest freely available alternative. It is not a replacement for PolySans in branded communications.

What to avoid

These typographic errors undermine legibility, brand consistency, and the clarity and consistency of Strategus communications.

  • Avoid extremely tall leading for both heading and body text
  • Avoid overly tight leading, with crashing ascenders and descenders, for both heading and body text
  • Avoid overly tight tracking, with crashing glyphs
  • Avoid overly wide tracking
  • Avoid mixed weights or fonts within a single headline

Photography

Landscape: dos

These are guiding principles when creating landscape photography assets. They're not hard and fast rules, but a starting point for shooting imagery used in print or digital collateral.

Shoot organic compositions where the subject matter does not feel staged.
Play with light and shadow to create visual interest.
Rich and warm color tones make the photography feel human and approachable.
Imperfect and natural colors express a grounded, authentic approach to civic spaces.
Show the architectural character and texture of Strategus's built environment.
Capture the density and diversity of the brand's neighborhoods at street level.
Photography

Landscape: don'ts

Avoid photography choices that undermine authenticity, flatten the brand's character, or project an aspirational image that does not reflect the lived experience of Strategus audiences.

Don't shoot desaturated, overly stylized landscapes that strip away the brand's warmth and character.
Avoid blown-out, over-exposed images that feel generic and lack a sense of place.
Don't apply heavy color grading or filters that make images feel processed and inauthentic.
Avoid generic stock skylines that could belong to any city, use photography that is specific to Strategus.
Don't shoot from helicopter or drone perspectives that make the brand feel abstract rather than lived-in.
Avoid overly polished real estate or promotional imagery that projects aspiration over reality.
